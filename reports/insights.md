# Key Insights

Findings from the Stack Overflow Developer Survey 2025 after cleaning (21,702 respondents with a valid annual salary).
All salary figures are in USD. Medians are used instead of means because salary data is right-skewed.

## 1. Typical salary

- The median annual salary is about **$81K**, while the mean is about **$97K**. The gap shows a long right tail of high earners.
- Salaries are self-reported and cluster on round numbers. For example, 262 respondents report exactly EUR 70,000 (about $81,210), which is why several country medians land on the same value.

## 2. Country matters more than anything else

- The median salary in the United States is **$150,000** (n = 4,818), versus **$19,180** in India: a **7.8x** gap.
- Among countries with at least 150 respondents, Switzerland ($142,592) and Israel ($141,188) follow the United States. Israel's estimate rests on only 163 respondents, so it is less stable.

## 3. Experience raises pay everywhere, but the effect differs by country

- In every country shown, salary rises with experience, fastest during the first 10 to 15 years, and then flattens.
- In the United States the median goes from about $82,500 (0-2 years) to $175,000 (21-30 years): roughly **2.1x**.
- In India it goes from about $7,000 to $57,000: roughly **8.2x**, so the gap with the United States shrinks from about 11.8x at entry level to about 3.1x at 21-30 years.
- Caution: this is cross-sectional data (different people at different career stages), not the career path of the same people.

## 4. Education has a modest association with pay

- Relative to their own country's median, respondents with a Master's or a doctorate/professional degree earn about **7% more** than those with a Bachelor's degree (1.07x vs 1.00x).
- Respondents with only secondary education earn about 0.85x of the national median.
- Education is correlated with experience and job role, so this is an association, not proof that a degree raises pay.

## 5. Job role

- Leadership and architecture roles earn the most relative to their own country's median: senior executives **1.60x** (n = 296), engineering managers **1.42x** (n = 692) and software architects **1.29x** (n = 1,553).
- The lowest relative pay is in system administration (**0.67x**, n = 211), data or business analysis (**0.71x**, n = 167) and academic research (**0.72x**, n = 406).
- AI/ML engineers (**1.17x**, n = 320) earn more than data scientists (**0.95x**, n = 317).
- The most common role, full-stack developer (n = 6,925), sits below the national median at **0.91x**.
- Seniority explains part of the leadership premium: executives, architects and engineering managers have a median of 17 to 20 years of experience, versus 12 overall. It does not explain everything: AI/ML engineers (median 8 years) and cloud infrastructure engineers (11 years) earn above the median, while system administrators (12 years) earn well below it.

## 6. Programming languages

- Respondents report about six languages each, so language groups overlap heavily.
- Among the 20 most used languages, Ruby (**1.16x**), Go (**1.13x**) and Swift (**1.13x**) are associated with the highest relative pay; PHP is the lowest (**0.87x**, n = 3,685).
- The most widespread languages (JavaScript, Python, SQL, Java) sit at about **1.00x**, which is expected because they define the typical respondent.
- Differences are mostly within +/- 15%, and language is likely a proxy for the type of company or work, not a cause of higher pay.

## 7. Work arrangement

- Fully remote respondents earn about **1.10x** their country's median, versus **0.75x** for fully in-person respondents.
- The pattern holds inside each of the five largest countries: in-person is the lowest-paid arrangement in all five, and remote is the highest or tied for highest.
- Example (median salary, remote vs in-person): United States $165,000 vs $116,000, United Kingdom $102,106 vs $68,071, Germany $92,812 vs $71,929.
- Remote respondents are more experienced (median 13 years vs 8 for in-person), so part of the gap reflects experience. In the United States the raw remote premium is about 42%; within experience groups it is smaller but persists: **+28%** (0-5 years), **+21%** (6-10), **+21%** (11-20) and **+10%** (21+).
- This is still an association: remote roles are concentrated in software companies, and some remote workers are employed by companies based in higher-paying countries.

## Data notes and limitations

- Only about half of all survey respondents reported a salary, so the results may be biased toward people who are comfortable sharing it.
- The sample is experienced (median 12 years of professional experience) and reflects Stack Overflow users, not all developers.
- The 2025 "Employed" category does not separate full-time from part-time work.
- Salaries are self-reported and cluster on round numbers, so headline figures are rounded.
- All comparisons are associations from survey data, not causal effects.
